# ValenOS — 5-minute pitch: speaker notes

Cue sheet for `ValenOS-Pitch-Deck.pptx`. Timings are a guide, not a script — the slide carries the visual thought, you carry the argument.


## 1. Hook

[0:00–0:25] Open on the question, then stop talking for a beat.
Every CRM you have ever used is a filing cabinet. You feed it, it remembers. That is the whole deal. We think that deal is finished.


## 2. The mess

[0:25–0:55] Nine systems, nine logins, nine half-pictures of the same customer.
The context that actually decides the deal — what was said on the call, what was promised in the email, what procurement is waiting on — is split across all of them. Nothing holds the whole relationship.


## 3. The old model

[0:55–1:20] Ask the room: who here has someone whose actual job is keeping the CRM clean?
That is the tell. The software is not working for you — you are working for the software. That is the problem we watched our own team have.


## 4. The new model

[1:20–1:40] This is the turn of the whole pitch. Say it slowly and let it land.
Agents sit inside the system, with the company’s full context, working alongside the team.


## 5. The proof — without/with context

[1:40–2:15] THE slide. Give them time to read both — say nothing for four or five seconds.
Left is what every AI sales tool on the market produces. Right is the same agent with the relationship behind it. And every line is cited back to its source, so people trust it enough to send it.


## 6. The context advantage

[2:15–2:40] This is the moat, and it is a boring one: we own the CRM, so we own the context.
A bolt-on assistant sees one prompt. Our agents see the whole relationship — and they all read the same context, which is what lets them hand work to each other.


## 7. How it works

[2:40–3:05] This is the architecture answer without a tech-stack slide.
Connected data gives deep context, agents act on it, workflows chain them together, and a human approval step sits wherever the team wants one. Autonomy is a dial, not a switch.


## 8. The product

[3:05–3:25] Let them look before you talk.
You open ValenOS in the morning and the list is already there, already scored, with the first email written. Your job is to say yes, no, or change this line — not to do the research.


## 9. Old vs new

[3:25–3:40] Don’t read the columns. Say the one-liner and move on:
Old CRM records what happened. ValenOS helps make what happens next.


## 10. Traction

[3:40–4:05] Be straight about the stage — it buys credibility for everything else.
Five companies are testing. None are paying yet. The Dale Carnegie Poland pilot is the one that tells us this is real. All of it exists four months after the first commit in June.


## 11. Founders

[4:05–4:20] Short and human. Thirty years of trust doesn’t show up on a cap table, but it is why two people can move this fast without breaking.


## 12. Founding members

[4:20–4:35] Founding member is not a discount — it is access to the two of us. We set it up with you, we answer when you write, and we build what you tell us is missing.


## 13. The raise

[4:35–4:50] State the terms once, plainly, and stop. Do not over-explain the raise.


## 14. Close

[4:50–5:05] Close on the invitation, not on an ask.
We are not looking for customers yet. We are looking for founding members — companies and backers who want to shape what a CRM becomes when agents are actually part of the company.
